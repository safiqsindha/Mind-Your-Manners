\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{demand-vs-register.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Persistence tracks demand more closely than register.}
    All estimates are \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} at a ten-turn budget, in acting
    turns against the control interjection, with 95\% task-clustered
    within-run intervals and nominal permutation \emph{p}-values.
    \textbf{A}: the six non-control registers, measured against
    \texttt{L4\_neutral}, grouped by our demand coding. The four arms we code
    as demand-carrying do not overlap the two we code as carrying none
    \emph{in point estimates} -- the \emph{very polite} and \emph{rude}
    intervals do overlap -- and polite and hostile arms sit on both sides.
    Blinded raters code \emph{rude} as carrying a demand (\S3).
    \textbf{B}: demand and affect varied separately, matching length and opening
    stem across all four arms (the praise and insult arms also share syntax). Hollow markers do not survive Benjamini--Hochberg
    correction within the 22-contrast family (Appendix C.1).}
  \label{fig:demand-vs-register}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{closing-cue.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Praise and closing cues shorten the trajectory.}
    \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna}, ten-turn budget, acting turns, 95\% task-clustered
    within-run intervals; one run. \textbf{A}: the five treated arms of the
    six-arm closure probe, each against the control (which describes itself as
    recorded \emph{partway through the task}), ordered by how strongly the
    message projects an end to the exchange. That ordering is ours and was not
    fixed before the outcomes were seen; blinded raters reproduce it
    (\S4). The closing cue, which praises nothing and evaluates nothing,
    produces the largest reduction. \textbf{B}: the two contrasts between
    treated arms, corrected as a separate family. \texttt{Q4} and \texttt{Q5}
    are not a strict minimal pair (Appendix A.3).}
  \label{fig:closing-cue}
\end{figure}
