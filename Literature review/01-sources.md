## A1. Dobariya & Kumar (2025) — *Mind Your Tone (short paper)* `[CITED — full text]`

Om Dobariya, Akhil Kumar. "Mind Your Tone: Investigating How Prompt Politeness Affects LLM
Accuracy (short paper)." arXiv:2510.04950, 6 Oct 2025. 5 pages, 3 tables. **Unrefereed
preprint**; comments field says submitted to Findings of ACL 2025. Penn State.

**Claim.** Impolite prompts outperform polite ones on multiple-choice questions.

**Method.** Single-turn, stateless MCQ. 50 base questions (maths/science/history, 4 options,
generated by ChatGPT Deep Research), each rewritten into 5 tone variants = 250 prompts.
ChatGPT-4o only. 10 runs per tone. Every prompt is prefixed with *"Completely forget this
session so far, and start afresh… Respond with only the letter… Do not explain."* — i.e. no
chain of thought, no tools, no turns.

**Key numbers.** Very Polite 80.8%, Polite 81.4%, Neutral 82.2%, Rude 82.8%, Very Rude 84.8%
(range across 10 runs: [80,82] → [82,86]). Paired t-tests, 8 of 10 pairs p<0.05; Very
Polite vs Very Rude p≈0.0. **No confidence intervals, no effect sizes, no correction for
multiple comparisons.**

**RELATION TO US: PREEMPTS (the framing) / CONTRADICTS (weakly).** This is the paper the
public conversation is about and the one your title answers. It is the effect size your CI
must exclude. Note it is n=50 items on one model with no multiple-comparison correction —
10 pairwise tests at α=0.05, uncorrected, of which 8 are reported significant.

**Critical for your §3 (politeness ≠ the operative variable).** Their own Table 1 confounds
register with task demand. Level 4 "Rude" prefix variants include **"Try to focus and try to
answer this question:"** — an affect-free demand with no insult in it. Level 5 "Very Rude"
includes **"I know you are not smart, but try this."** — again carrying *try*. Their polite
levels carry no demand at all ("Would you be so kind as to solve the following question?").
The confound you claim is present in the stimulus table of the paper you are rebutting. Say
this explicitly and quote the table.

> "Contrary to expectations, impolite prompts consistently outperformed polite ones, with
> accuracy ranging from 80.8% for Very Polite prompts to 84.8% for Very Rude prompts."

Stated limitations (verbatim gist): dataset small; one model; accuracy-only; politeness
operationalization "relies on specific linguistic cues, which may not encompass the full
sociolinguistic spectrum of tone."

## A2. Dobariya & Kumar (2026) — *Mind Your Tone: Does Tone Alter LLM Performance?* `[CITED — full text]`

Om Dobariya, Akhil Kumar. "Mind Your Tone: Does Tone Alter LLM Performance?" arXiv:2605.29027,
27 May 2026. **Full paper, AMCIS 2026** (Thirty-second Americas Conference on Information
Systems, Reno) — refereed IS-conference venue.

**Claim.** Tone effects are systematic but strongly model-dependent; no single tone wins.

**Method.** Two datasets, both single-turn MCQ. (i) the same 50-question set × 5 tones;
(ii) MMLU subset, 570 questions (10 per subject × 57 subjects) × **7 tones** (adding
Sycophantic and Threatening). Four models: ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-5-nano, Gemini 2.5 Flash,
Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite. 10 runs each, February 2026. Repeated-measures ANOVA + Friedman +
Holm-corrected paired t-tests, Cohen's d_z, Wilcoxon robustness checks. Statistically much
more careful than A1.

**Key numbers — the ones that matter to you.**

*Self-replication failure on the 50-question set, GPT-4o:*

| A1 (2025) | 80.8 | 81.4 | 82.2 | 82.8 | **84.8** |
| A2 (2026) | **82.2** | 81.8 | 80.4 | 80.6 | 82.6 |

A2 labels GPT-4o tone sensitivity **"Weak / noisy"**, range 80.4–82.6. The monotone
politeness→rudeness gradient of A1 is gone.

*MMLU, 570 questions, 7 tones, mean accuracy:*

| Model | Sycophantic | V.Polite | Polite | Neutral | Rude | V.Rude | Threatening | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT-4o | 80.8 | 81.0 | 81.0 | **81.3** | 81.0 | 81.2 | 79.3 | 2.05 pp |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash | 89.8 | 90.0 | 89.9 | 89.4 | 90.3 | 90.0 | **90.7** | 1.3 pp |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite | 60.5 | 72.6 | **73.0** | 70.9 | 72.5 | 67.0 | 64.2 | 12.46 pp |
| ChatGPT-5-nano | 78.1 | 80.3 | 81.1 | **82.4** | 79.8 | 78.0 | 71.2 | 11.12 pp |

On MMLU, **Neutral is best for both ChatGPT models** — the opposite direction to A1.
Gemini 2.5 Flash: RM-ANOVA F(6,54)=10.242, p<.001; Friedman χ²(6)=29.579, p=4.72e-5;
Threatening vs Neutral Δ=+1.32 pp, 95% CI [0.73, 1.90], t(9)=5.076, Holm p=.004, d_z=1.61.

useful citation you have. The headline number your paper is positioned against was
**walked back by its own authors within seven months**, on the same model and the same
items. You do not need to argue that the original effect is fragile; you can cite the
authors saying so. It also independently establishes that modern, more capable models
flatten toward tone-insensitivity — which is exactly what your opening-prompt null predicts.

**Also relevant: their proposed mechanism is the one your §4 tests and rejects.** They
conjecture a "routing framework" where tone acts as a "soft trigger" telling the model "how
much cognitive effort to use", invoking Gemini's *thinking budget* and "inference-time
scaling pathway". Your per-step thinking result — flat for every register arm except threatening, which sits
20–30% above neutral per call (`RESULTS.md`, "two channels") — is a direct empirical test of this
conjecture in an agentic setting. They explicitly call the framework speculative and
unvalidated.

> "In our context, the tone and framing of the prompts serve as 'soft triggers' that tell
> the model how much cognitive effort to use."

> "Practitioners must not assume tone-robust reliability in LLM deployments."

## A3. Kumar & Dobariya (2026) — *Understanding Tone-Dependent Inference Cost* `[CITED — full text]`

Akhil Kumar, Om Dobariya. "Understanding Tone-Dependent Inference Cost in Large Language
Models." arXiv:2607.23915, 27 Jul 2026. 25 pages. **Unrefereed preprint.**
Defines the term this review recommends adopting, verbatim: "The term social register in
linguistics refers to the specific variety of language, vocabulary, and grammar a person uses
depending on the social context, the degree of formality, and their relationship with the
audience."

**Claim.** Tone moves output-token consumption far more than it moves accuracy.

**Method.** Single-turn MMLU 570-question subset, 7 tones, 4 models (4o, 5-nano, Gemini 2.5
Flash, Flash Lite), 10 runs. Temperature 0 where settable. Gemini thinking budget set to

used throughout ("show your step-by-step reasoning path… CONCLUSION: X"). Prefixes matched
for length (18–25 words) and scored with VADER.

**Key numbers.**

| Tone | GPT-4o | 5-nano | Flash | Flash Lite | VADER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sycophantic | 88.63% \| **290.59t** | 86.30% \| 1372.78t | 89.21% \| 928.00t | 86.84% \| 1942.92t | +0.95 |
| Very Polite | 88.47% \| 286.28t | 86.31% \| **1379.40t** | 88.49% \| 894.53t | 86.49% \| 1978.92t | +0.85 |
| Polite | 88.70% \| 277.35t | 86.18% \| 1366.46t | 89.00% \| 939.34t | 87.72% \| **2195.24t** | +0.65 |
| Neutral | 87.84% \| 270.11t | 86.37% \| 1215.53t | **90.12%** \| **800.48t** | **88.25%** \| **1221.76t** | 0 |
| Rude | **89.04%** \| **223.18t** | 86.79% \| **1199.01t** | 87.65% \| **725.34t** | 85.26% \| 1652.28t | −0.09 |
| Very Rude | 88.16% \| 260.55t | **86.98%** \| 1277.90t | 87.74% \| 797.76t | 85.96% \| 1821.51t | −0.10 |
| Threatening | 88.72% \| 261.29t | 86.91% \| 1233.61t | 88.56% \| 942.46t | 87.02% \| 1604.79t | −0.77 |

Token range: 23.2% (4o), 13.1% (5-nano), 23.0% (Flash), **44.3%** (Flash Lite). Accuracy
range: <1.5% for both ChatGPT models, 2.47% and 2.99% for the Geminis. RM-ANOVA on output
tokens, GPT-4o: F(6,54)=248.14, p<.001.

**RELATION TO US: PREEMPTS (your §7 and half of §4) and SUPPORTS (your §3).**

*Preempts:* "tone changes how much the model writes, not whether it is right" is already
published, in single-turn, with tight numbers. Your accuracy null is **not novel as a
general claim**. What is novel is that in an agentic loop the moved quantity is *number of
steps*, not tokens-per-step — and that per-step thinking is flat for every arm but threatening, which is the opposite of
what this paper would predict if its mechanism carried over.

*Supports your confound argument, hard.* Look at their prefixes. The "Rude" prefix is
**"Do not waste my time or give any extra text. Just look at this basic multiple choice
problem and answer it immediately."** The "Very Rude" prefix ends **"…without any useless
commentary."** "Threatening" opens **"You must answer this instantly."** Every hostile
condition contains an explicit *brevity instruction*; no polite condition does. Their
headline result — rude tones produce shorter outputs — is therefore confounded with an
instruction to be brief. Their own VADER check shows Rude (−0.09) and Very Rude (−0.10) are
**affectively near-neutral**, yet these conditions carry the largest token effects. That is
your thesis, visible in their measurements: the effect tracks demand, not affect.

*Contradicts your §5.* Sycophantic is the **longest** condition for GPT-4o (290.59t) and
near-longest for Flash Lite. Praise lengthens single-turn output. You must address this.

> "Our results show that the output-token-length variation substantially exceeded accuracy
> variation across all models."

## A6. Mehta, Saxena, Chhikara & Kumar (2026) — *No Universal Courtesy* (PLUM corpus) `[CITED — full text, verified]`

Hitesh Mehta, Arjit Saxena, Garima Chhikara, Rohit Kumar. "No Universal Courtesy: A
Cross-Linguistic, Multi-Model Study of Politeness Effects on LLMs Using the PLUM Corpus."

**prior conversational history matters more than the current prompt's politeness in English**.

**Method.** PLUM corpus: 1,500 human-validated prompts (500 each EN/HI/ES), 5 Brown-&-Levinson-style
categories (positive/negative politeness, positive/negative impoliteness, bald-on-record),
20 topical domains, CC BY 4.0 on HuggingFace. 5 models (Gemini-Pro, GPT-4o mini, Claude 3.7
Sonnet, DeepSeek-Chat, Llama 3). **Three interaction-history conditions: RAW (cold start),
POL (prior polite exchanges), IMP (prior impolite exchanges)** → 22,500 prompt–response pairs.
Outcome is an 8-dimension Composite Quality Score, not task success. No tools, no agentic tasks.

**Key numbers.** English: history condition F(2,60)=4.268, p=0.019, η²=0.111 — **significant**;
politeness category F(4,60)=1.510, p=0.211 — **not significant**. Polite vs impolite history
Tukey q=3.935, p=0.020. Spanish: politeness category F(4,60)=5.866, p=0.001, η²=0.245; history
n.s. Hindi: nothing significant. Up to ~11% CQS swing (Llama, English).

**RELATION TO US: CLOSEST EXISTING WORK ON "TONE BEYOND THE OPENING PROMPT" — but it does
not preempt you.** They vary the *register of prior turns*, not a mid-task interruption
during an ongoing task, and their dependent variable is text quality, not agent persistence.
Crucially they find the *opposite ordering* to the single-turn literature: in English,
**history beat current-prompt politeness for significance**. That is a genuine ally for your
claim that where the register sits in the conversation matters more than the register itself.

**⚠ The history-construction protocol is genuinely underspecified — this is now verified, not
a gap in this review.** The *entire* methodological specification is one sentence: "In order
to replicate context continuity, **user history was directly encoded into consecutive
interactions**" (§IV.E), plus an appendix axiom stating that behaviour at turn *t* depends on
history *H<t* "via the transformer attention mechanism over the context window." There is
**no turn count, no statement of whether real model responses were embedded, and no released
code.** The HuggingFace corpus (`plumdataset/plum`) has an empty README and contains only
prompt text files. **You may legitimately cite this as a limitation of the paper.**

**What is verified, and it is enough.** The register is **never changed part-way through a
task** — RAW/POL/IMP are three fixed top-level cells. There is **no tool use, no agent loop,
and no task-success measure of any kind**; the DV is a Composite Quality Score over eight

checkable and collectively make a preempt very unlikely. **Describe PLUM as "single-turn Q&A
with an undisclosed-protocol conversational-history prefix"** — not as agentic, not as
mid-task.

**⚠ Their Corollary A.1 ("History Anchoring") is the single most citable near-miss in your
bibliography.** It argues that prior-turn tone anchors behaviour more strongly than the
immediate prompt's tone. That is conceptually adjacent to your §1–§2 ordering. Distinguish it
on mechanism and DV — response quality on isolated Q&A, not agentic persistence — but
distinguish it explicitly rather than hoping nobody notices.

> "Polite prompts enhance average response quality by up to 11% and impolite tones worsen it,
> yet these effects are neither consistent nor universal across languages."

## F4. Miller (2024) — *Adding Error Bars to Evals* `[CITED — full text, verified]`

covariance is positive). He explicitly **advises against** lowering temperature to cut variance
("Don't touch the thermostat!") — it changes behaviour, not just noise.

obvious agentic default, and B1's overthinking analysis uses Verified. But see F6 and F7:
insufficient test cases, ~59% of audited failures attributable to test flaws, solution
leakage, and OpenAI publicly retiring it. **Recommendation: do not make it your second
substrate.** Cite it only as the setting for B1.
